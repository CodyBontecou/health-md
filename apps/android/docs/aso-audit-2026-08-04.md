# Android ASO audit — August 4th 2026

## Verdict

**ASO health: 6/10.** Health.md already ranks well for its main English search terms. The larger gaps are conversion, social proof, creative freshness and localization.

The strongest signal is the title. Keep it. In a logged-out US-English Play search snapshot, Health.md ranked second for “health data export” and “export health data android”, and third for “health connect export”, despite showing only 100+ downloads and no public aggregate rating.

The most urgent problems are:

1. The public full description says users receive **three** free exports while the public release notes and app say **10**.
2. The core export screenshot is fifth, not first.
3. Phone screenshots are polished but below Google’s 1080×1920 promotional recommendation, repeat too much branding and show a stale 99/99 metric count. The current catalog contains 106 metrics.
4. Only `en-US` store metadata is versioned even though the app ships in English plus 15 translated locales.
5. The public listing does not show an aggregate rating. The direct rival has 1K+ downloads and 38 ratings.

## Scope

This audit covers:

- the public US-English Google Play listing for `com.healthmd.android`;
- public, logged-out Play search results;
- local listing copy and assets at commit `d7a71d75c`;
- the current Android feature set and review-prompt code;
- Google’s current preview-asset and localization guidance.

Search rank is a snapshot, not a stable position. It varies by country, language, device, account and time. This audit does not include private Play Console visitor, acquisition, conversion, retention or revenue data.

Health.md combines distinctive file formats, Obsidian workflows and polished phone creative. Its listing does not present those advantages quickly enough.

Public competitor reviews also reveal useful language and pain points: users value portable access, Google Sheets or CSV, automatic exports, historical data and responsive support. They complain about unreliable background exports and limited control over high-frequency data. Health.md should stress previewable output, granular-or-summary control and reliable export history without claiming a complete provider-database backup.

## Copy findings

### Keep the title

`Health.md – Health Data Export` uses all 30 characters, contains the highest-intent phrase and already earns strong ranks. A title change would add risk without a clear gain.

### Test a more specific short description

Use the current short description as the control. Test one variant at a time:

- **Variant A — 80 characters:** `Export Health Connect data to Markdown, CSV, JSON or Obsidian files you control.`
- **Variant B — 77 characters:** `Archive Health Connect data as private Markdown, CSV, JSON or Obsidian files.`

Variant A best addresses the weak CSV, JSON and Obsidian query positions while stating the product plainly. It should be tested for conversion rather than assumed to win.

### Rebuild the first half of the full description

The present description devotes 38% of its copy to the FHIR section and another 17% to privacy. That detail helps permission review, but it delays the main reasons to install.

The first 600–900 characters should cover:

1. Health Connect data exported to Markdown, CSV, JSON, NDJSON and Obsidian Bases;
2. user-selected local folders, vaults and endpoints;
3. more than 100 selectable metrics;
4. file preview before export;
5. manual and scheduled exports;
6. four home-screen widgets;
7. 10 free manual exports and a one-time unlock.

Retain the medical-category list and disclaimer lower in the description. Do not reuse the old “only app” claim from `docs/aso-benefits.md`; the competitive search does not support it.

## Creative findings

### Phone screenshots

The current order is:

1. Fine-tune every export
2. Preview before you export
3. Track every export
4. Automate every export
5. Export your wellness data

This sequence puts the core job last. Move the current fifth concept to the first position while new assets are prepared.

The next creative set should use at least 1080×1920 images and this order:

1. **Export Health Connect data** — Markdown, CSV, JSON and Obsidian files you control
2. **Health data in your Obsidian vault** — daily notes and Bases
3. **Choose from 100+ metrics** — sleep, activity, heart, vitals and medical records
4. **Preview every file first** — inspect content, path and structure
5. **Automate your health archive** — scheduled exports and reliable history
6. **Health widgets at a glance** — activity, sleep, heart and summary widgets
7. **Connect your own tools** — selected folders, HTTPS API or paired desktop CLI

Design changes:

- Give the real interface most of each frame, especially in the first three screenshots.
- Use one headline and one short support line. Remove the four small feature cards, which are illegible at listing size.
- Reduce the repeated logo block. Google may show these assets together, so repeated branding wastes space.
- Raise contrast and use more violet surface area; the five nearly black thumbnails merge into Play’s dark theme.
- Remove the airplane-mode status icon and keep the status bar neutral.
- Avoid brittle exact counts such as 99/99. The current app catalog has 106 metrics; “100+” will age better.

### Larger-device screenshots

The live 1200×1920 and 1600×2560 sets use older 60+ metric creative and phone-shaped UI. Replace them with current, genuine large-screen captures if the app is distributed to tablets. Google recommends at least four 9:16 screenshots of 1080px or more for large screens and advises against extra marketing text that can be cut off.

### Icon and feature graphic

The low-poly heart is memorable and cleaner than direct competitors’ icons. Keep it as the control, then test a version with a brighter full-bleed violet background because the black square blends into dark Play surfaces.

The tracked icon is an RGB PNG without an alpha channel. Google’s current asset guide asks for a 32-bit 512×512 PNG with alpha. Regenerate a compliant source even though Play currently serves the existing file.

No `featureGraphic.png` is tracked under `play-console/graphics/en-US/`. Google requires a 1024×500 feature graphic. The published app must therefore depend on a Console-only asset that this repository cannot reproduce or audit. Export it, version it and test a brighter, centered concept with little or no text.

## Ratings and reviews

The public page has no aggregate rating. This matters more than another round of keyword changes.

The app already requests an in-app review after two fully successful exports. Two implementation details weaken that loop:

- `ExportViewModel` records `hasRequestedReview` before `requestReviewFlow()` succeeds.
- The boolean is permanent, although Google can suppress the dialog because of quota or context.

Use a dated attempt with a long cooldown instead of a permanent one-shot flag. Ask only after a meaningful success, never pre-screen for positive sentiment and respect Google’s time-bound quota. At minimum, do not burn the only attempt when `requestReviewFlow()` fails.

Track successful-export-to-review-prompt eligibility, request failures and public rating count without collecting review sentiment or health data.

## Localization

The app contains English plus 15 translated UI locales, but the repository contains only an `en-US` Play listing. This leaves store acquisition behind the product.

Use Play Console acquisition data to choose the first languages. A practical first batch, because the app already has matching resources, is:

- German (`de-DE`)
- Spanish (`es-419` and/or `es-ES`)
- French (`fr-FR`)
- Portuguese (`pt-BR`)
- Japanese (`ja-JP`)

Localize title suffixes, short and full descriptions, screenshot overlays and the one-time product copy. Machine translation is a useful draft, not final marketing copy. Have a native speaker review health, privacy and medical terms.

## Validation and release hygiene

The local metadata is within Play limits:

- title: 30/30;
- short description: 71/80;
- full description: 3,228/4,000;
- phone screenshots: five of eight allowed.

A canonical temporary Fastlane layout passed `gplay validate listing` and `gplay validate screenshots`.

The repository’s custom paths can, however, produce false-positive validation. Running the same commands directly against `play-console/listing` inspected only the title, while direct screenshot validation returned an empty locale object. Either adopt the canonical names (`short_description.txt`, `full_description.txt`, `images/phoneScreenshots/`) or add an explicit conversion-and-validation task to CI.

Consolidate or retire stale ASO source files:

- `docs/aso-benefits.md` still says 60+ metrics and calls Health.md the only local exporter;
- `docs/aso-screenshot-pairings.md` still says 61/61 metrics;
- `PLAY_CONSOLE_BROWSER_PROMPT.md` still describes v1.5.2 and an older screenshot plan.

## Success measures

Record a baseline before changing the listing. Judge the work by:

- store-listing conversion rate, overall and by traffic source;
- first-time acquisitions from search;
- rank for the 10 queries above;
- public rating count and average rating;
- conversion by language and country;
- successful first export and second successful export rates.

Do not judge an experiment only by impressions or keyword count.

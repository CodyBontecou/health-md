# Native subscriptions, archive retirement and large exports: checked facts

Status: design research for [Q35–Q56 and the next frontier](production-design-interview.md), not an accepted architecture, provider qualification or implementation/deployment authorization. Sources were checked on 2026-10-05. No private accounts, transactions, credentials, real health data or live Health.md services were used. The disposable pilot and shipped mobile contracts remain unchanged.

## Native billing is compatible with existing lifetime purchases

- Apple's [auto-renewable subscription guide](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/) explicitly permits subscriptions alongside other in-app purchase types and describes subscription groups, durations, upgrades/downgrades and server notifications. A Cloud subscription need not replace Health.md's lifetime native-app products. Its recommendation of one group for one subscription helps prevent duplicate Apple subscriptions, but does not prevent a separate Play/web purchase.
- The same guide documents standard first-year proceeds of 70% of the price, minus applicable taxes, increasing to 85% after a year of paid service. Enrolled [Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/) developers receive 85%, minus applicable taxes. No Health.md enrollment or fee eligibility was verified here.
- Google's [service-fee table](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en) lists 15% for automatically renewing subscriptions. Actual contracts, taxes, settlement delay, refunds and any eligible programs still require review.
- Apple's [payment guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#payments) and Google's [Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en) govern the actual app/account/purchase flow. Q38 selects native checkout and prefers web too if permissible; it does not establish that a web link or purchase route is approved. US residence is not storefront eligibility.

Consequences: qualify StoreKit and Play Billing separately with backend-verified account-bound entitlements; prevent overlapping purchases across channels, not just within a store. Monthly/annual decimal tiers and a 10 GB lifetime-linked benefit/no general free tier are selected. Prices, annual discount, program/benefit eligibility, stacking and separate introductory trials remain open. Annual cash receipts must be modeled against the service owed over the paid term, not treated as recurring monthly income. Q56 withdraws the earlier $100 subsidy-plus-net-revenue spending envelope. Fees, settlement delay and annual service obligations remain factual pricing/capacity inputs; profitability is the owner's assumption, not verified evidence or a reinstated ceiling. Never send health contents/dates, archive identifiers or ingest/agent secrets to billing metadata or logs.

## Cancellation, store grace and archive-retirement grace differ

- Apple's [Billing Grace Period documentation](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-subscriptions/enable-billing-grace-period-for-auto-renewable-subscriptions/) permits 3, 16 or 28 days for monthly/yearly subscriptions. Grace retains subscription benefits during eligible payment recovery, requires renewal-state verification and is configured per app, not per product. No grace setting was inspected or changed.
- Google's [subscription lifecycle](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/lifecycle/subscriptions) says a cancelled subscription retains benefits through its paid expiry. Grace-period subscriptions retain benefits; account hold does not retain subscription entitlement. Current documented default payment recovery totals 60 days minus the configured grace period for account hold, with configuration options. Even a zero-day grace configuration has a minimum one-day silent grace; a stale local expiry is not an authoritative lapse signal.
- The Play lifecycle also documents successful recovery from hold, reusing the purchase token and restoring entitlement. Billing may recover after a proposed 30-day Health.md archive-retirement deadline, depending on the chosen clock. Restored purchase entitlement cannot restore exports that have already been erased.

Consequences: Q48 selects verified paid-access expiry including applicable store grace; Q49 preserves downloads/existing MCP/security/deletion during the 30-day archive grace, not unentitled uploads. Q51 selects archive-only erasure/revoked old grants with minimal account/control state retained. Q52 selects email and persistent app/dashboard notices at days 0/7/21/29 and holds irreversible retirement when Health.md cannot attempt them. Q53 separates verified primary erasure from provider-copy expiry/transcripts. Q50 rejects automatic still-paid over-quota retirement in favor of prior deliberate usage reduction. Lifetime-linked continuity, external-store reductions and detailed refund/revocation handling remain unresolved. This is **not** deletion 30 days after someone disables auto-renewal. Reconcile current verified store state before any irreversible action; duplicate, late or out-of-order events must not erase an active subscriber's data. Do not infer that a store's payment warning satisfies Health.md's archive-deletion warning. Uncertainty in authoritative billing state must not be treated as verified nonpayment.

Health.md's current [lifecycle](../src/lifecycle.ts) handles account deletion and age-based removal of unreferenced revisions, not subscription retirement. The blocked [maintenance configuration](../wrangler.maintenance.toml) has a 30-day revision policy. That number is neither Q40's subscription grace nor D1's separate history window; reusing it would expire revisions belonging to active subscribers. No billing lifecycle or subscription-retirement job is implemented by this documentation.

## Large logical exports still require bounded processing

| Checked surface | Fact | Consequence for Q43 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workers | Current [limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/) list 128 MB per isolate and finite request-body limits determined by the Cloudflare account plan, independently of the Workers plan. The page recommends streaming instead of whole-body buffering. | Removing `MAX_EXPORT_BYTES`, paying for Workers or calling a request an app upload does not make processing unbounded or US-qualified. |
| R2 | Current [limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/platform/limits/) list 10,000 multipart parts, a roughly 5 TiB object ceiling and a 5 GiB single-part ceiling; [multipart documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/objects/multipart-objects/) defines part constraints and completion/abort behavior. | Provider multipart storage is not a ready native protocol, permission to expose object URLs, or proof of end-to-end bounded validation/encryption/reading. Logical artifacts need not map to one provider object. |
| Existing Cloud writer | [Ingestion](../src/exports.ts) reads a bounded body, validates the compatibility envelope, hashes it and passes whole plaintext to [encryption](../src/crypto.ts). | The current 25 MiB compatibility route cannot meet a no-arbitrary-total-app-export-cap requirement by a flag change. Preserve that legacy route and pilot. |
| Existing reads | [MCP reader](../mcp/reader.ts) decrypts complete exports and JSON-parses them for navigation/aggregates; [owner downloads](../src/exports.ts) and [portability](../src/account-export.ts) also materialize an envelope. | Bounded output pages do not establish bounded memory per original artifact. Large native uploads must also be readable, portable and erasable without whole-artifact buffering. |
| Native contracts | [Apple API export](../../apple/docs/features/api-endpoint-export.md) sends compatibility JSON including selected supported lossless fields; an indivisible day can exceed a batch bound. [Android API export](../../android/docs/api-endpoint-export.md) also exposes distinct provider-native raw JSON/NDJSON. | Q46 settles no arbitrary whole-export cap within allowance and Q47 selects JSON only. Exact JSON schema families remain open; raw JSON is not selected merely by excluding NDJSON. Do not rewrite schemas, lower detail or fabricate typed parity. |
| Raw restart semantics | Android's [raw-snapshot v1 contract](../../android/docs/export-contract/raw-snapshot-v1.md) requires a final verified manifest; incomplete files are not snapshots. Capture restart is from scratch, not continuation at a provider consistency point. | If raw Cloud support is selected, resumable delivery of an immutable completed artifact is distinct from resuming provider capture. Review native secure spool/lifecycle rules; preserve existing custom-API behavior. |

Selected interpretation in Q46: no arbitrary cap on a valid logical native export within remaining purchased allowance; use finite authenticated chunks, bounded memory/concurrency and verified commit/read/download paths. Q47 selects JSON original artifacts, not arbitrary JSON, NDJSON or every app file. Exact schema families and Q45's included-capacity transitions still need decisions; authenticated chunk framing/encrypted storage are separate from original artifact format. Server authority comes from the enrolled write-only device grant and current account/plan state, never a claimed app header. Integrity, quota reservations, duplicate/overlapping chunks, credential/account switching, abort/restart, missing finalization and permanent-loss behavior require an independently versioned cross-platform contract and synthetic fixtures. Encrypted staging and indexes remain within the isolated US health-data boundary; no public/signed object URL, bearer secret in a URL, plaintext health spool in backups, or silent partial promotion.

This is not infinite storage, an unbounded HTTP request, a new interpretation of old receipts, or authorization to implement a protocol. Any physical/runtime bound must be truthful; a whole-export ceiling that prevents a valid within-allowance app export would conflict with Q46. Chunked transport must preserve original artifact bytes and supported capture states.

## Lifetime-linked allowance requires a separate verified benefit model

The owner's Q45 selects 10 GB included Cloud storage for users with lifetime rights, not a general free tier or guarantee of lifetime hosting. Public source does not identify which rights qualify for this new benefit:

- Apple [Full Access](../../apple/docs/features/full-access-unlock.md) and [purchase source](../../apple/HealthMd/Shared/Managers/PurchaseManager.swift) include individual/family purchases, family-shared ownership and legacy paid-app paths, with local cached entitlement. A family purchase may fund separate eligible adults' accounts if selected; it does not permit health-archive pooling under Q8.
- Google Play [lifetime access](../../android/docs/features/lifetime-unlock.md) uses one-time purchase/current and cached entitlement. [F-Droid distribution](../../android/docs/distribution-channels.md) includes native full access without a purchase/restore flow. Generic authorized API access under Q55 and free-benefit eligibility are distinct questions.
- Apple's [AppTransaction ID documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/storekit/apptransaction/apptransactionid) says the ID is stable for the Apple Account/app across redownloads, refunds, repurchases and storefront changes; family members have distinct IDs. It is also available without any in-app purchase. Therefore it is a potential reviewed binding input, **not proof of lifetime purchase**, verified personhood or entitlement validity. Legacy/device/API-version compatibility still requires review.
- Google's [billing security guidance](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/security) directs backend purchase-token verification, account/replay/eligibility checks and appropriate handling of pending and linked purchases before granting entitlements. Adapt those controls to previously acknowledged lifetime purchases; do not consume/rebuy a non-consumable or overwrite native rights merely to claim the benefit. No real purchase was verified here.

Eligibility, proof binding/rebinding, duplicate claims, family-member distinction, paid-capacity stacking/floor and paid-to-included continuation remain owner decisions. Existing local caches, a signed app-download identifier or an app header cannot become free Cloud authority. A purchase/claim never silently creates an account, uploads health data, broadens capture permissions or issues agent-read authority. Loss/uncertainty of authoritative proof needs reviewed fail-closed admission and deletion safeguards, not irreversible retirement based on an unavailable store query.

A 10 GB benefit alongside a paid 1 GB plan needs truthful offers and transition rules. Do not sell redundant capacity, infer stacking, automatically retire an archive covered by a valid benefit, or assume free capacity is physically reserved. Model actual included/paid usage and service obligations without claiming lifetime recovery or reinstating the withdrawn $100 ceiling.

## Store-managed changes limit usage-dependent downgrade blocking

Q50 requires prior deliberate archive reduction and rejects the proposed automatic retirement of still-paid over-quota archives. The actual stores own some plan-management paths:

- Apple's [subscription guide](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/) says users can upgrade/crossgrade/downgrade through App Store account settings; lower-level subscriptions ordinarily take effect at the next renewal. Its [consumer support guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/108131) documents switching plans through account.apple.com and system/account settings, outside Health.md.
- Google's [subscription management guide](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/subscriptions) documents Play management links, cancellation/resubscription/pause and replacement modes, including deferred tier changes. Qualify the actual catalog/Billing API surface rather than infer Apple-like timing for every Play change.

These facts show that blocking Health.md's own downgrade button cannot establish a universal store veto conditioned on Cloud bytes. The precise externally effective reduction policy is unselected; do not silently adopt whole-archive retirement, force continuing charges, misreport a store entitlement or promise to prevent cancellation. Pending changes, quota reservations, concurrent uploads and actual effective store state need independent qualification.

Owner-controlled reduction is also new work, not the existing account-deletion route. Define explicit original/revision selection, affected coverage/current pointers, dependent derivatives, bounded verified primary erasure and quota release without rewriting original bytes or choosing evidence to evict. Preserve existing permissions and upload receipt/capture semantics. Recommendations in Q57–Q65 remain unanswered.

## A US-region VM is a candidate, not residency evidence

AWS's [Data Privacy FAQ](https://aws.amazon.com/compliance/data-privacy-faq/) says customers choose the Regions storing their content and AWS will not move/replicate it outside the chosen Regions without agreement; its detailed answer includes service-initiated and lawful-order exceptions. It distinguishes customer content from provider account information and management identifiers/tags, and warns against putting sensitive information in those identifiers. These claims do not qualify all APIs, TLS termination, processors, key custody, support/monitoring metadata or Health.md's actual deployment.

The [EBS snapshot guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ebs/latest/userguide/ebs-snapshots.html) says EBS does not automatically back up volume data; snapshots must be created/configured, and snapshot data is replicated across Availability Zones within its Region. This is not evidence that an account has no snapshots, other provider copies or automated backup policy. Q26 permits D1 history only, not new EBS snapshots or Health.md backup jobs.

A fresh region-pinned VM with reviewed US HTTPS ingress remains an investigation candidate alongside a qualified localized Cloudflare topology. No provider, region, price quote, copy/deletion promise or capacity envelope is selected here. Keep all pilot, website, Practice and Wake resources/credentials separate. Review actual provider-copy expiry and no-resurrection before saying what Q40's day-30 primary retirement erases; delivered agent transcripts are outside Health.md's deletion control.
